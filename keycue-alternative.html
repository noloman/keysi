KeyCue alternative

A KeyCue alternative that's free for what KeyCue does.

KeyCue has been around a long time and it's a real, actively maintained product, and this page isn't going to pretend otherwise. But it's $39.99, and if what you want is "show me this app's shortcuts, and let me act on them," Keysi does that for nothing at all, and that part is free, with no time limit, no account and no card. The $19.99 is for coaching on top of it.

Both are worth a week of your time. Keysi's cheat sheet is free, and every install starts with 14 days of Pro.

Price, first, because it's the biggest difference

KeyCue is $39.99 as a one-time purchase covering two devices and no future major versions, or $19.99/yr for three. The Keysi feature that competes with it (hold a modifier, see every shortcut of the app you're in, click one to run it) is free, with no account, no card and no expiry. Keysi Pro is $19.99, once, covering three Macs, with free updates and no subscription tier at all, and it buys coaching rather than reference. So the honest comparison is either free against $39.99 for the same job, or a quarter of KeyCue's up-front price for strictly more, and the gap widens if you'd otherwise be renewing.

And if you use it for work, KeyCue's own pricing page puts you on a different plan. Both consumer tiers are labelled “Private use only” — the $39.99 one-time (“One-time payment for the current major version. Private use only.”) and the $19.99/yr (“Subscription ideal for individuals. Private use only.”). Professional use is the Business plan, at $39.99 per user per year. Those are their words, read from ergonis.com/keycue/pricing on 15 September 2026; check it yourself, because prices move.

Keysi has no such split. It's $19.99 once whether you open it at home or at a desk someone pays you to sit at. Against the plan a working professional actually needs, it pays for itself inside six months and then costs nothing again.

Being direct about what that buys: Keysi is a newer, smaller product from one developer. It's cheap because it's one focused tool, not because it's a stripped-down trial of something bigger.

Four things Keysi does that KeyCue's site doesn't claim

  • Click a row to run it. Keysi reads the live menu tree via the Accessibility API, so a row isn't a label: clicking it fires that exact menu action and the overlay dismisses.
  • Conflict warnings. Keysi reads macOS's own system shortcuts and flags any app shortcut the system will intercept first, so you find out before you press it rather than after nothing happens.
  • Coach mode. Keysi notices when you pick a command from the menu with the mouse that already has a keyboard shortcut, and offers one quiet, dismissible reminder, then tracks whether it stuck, so it stops surfacing the ones you've learned. Every other tool in this category, KeyCue included, waits to be asked.
  • Export the whole sheet. Keysi writes any app's shortcuts out as Markdown, a printable PDF, or a desktop picture sized to your screen, so the answer can leave Keysi and go on a wall, into a wiki, or behind your windows. Part of Keysi Pro.

"Doesn't claim" is the precise wording, and it's deliberate. These are features KeyCue's own website doesn't mention as of 2026-09-09, with the export row re-checked against ergonis.com/en/keycue and its features page on 2026-09-13, which isn't the same as proof they're absent. If you rely on one of them, check KeyCue's current site rather than taking a competitor's word for it.

Where KeyCue is the better choice

There are real cases, and it's worth naming them rather than leaving you to find out after buying:

  • You're not on macOS 14 yet. Keysi requires macOS 14 or later and simply won't run on older versions.
  • You want a long, established track record. KeyCue has years of releases behind it. Keysi is at 1.0. The changelog is the only honest evidence on offer, and right now it's short.

One honest limitation, stated up front

You can't type to search while holding ⌘. A global event monitor can observe keystrokes but not consume them, so anything typed would still reach the app underneath, and Keysi won't install the kind of input tap that could change that. Press the global hotkey (⇧⌘K by default) instead and the same content opens as a real panel, where search, arrow keys, and Escape all work. It's a deliberate trade, not an oversight.

Try it against KeyCue directly

Run them side by side for a week on the app you actually live in. that's a more useful test than any comparison table, this one included. KeyCue has a trial; Keysi doesn't need one for this comparison, because the part you'd be comparing never expires. Installs do start with 14 days of Keysi Pro on top, and when that ends the app drops to the free tier rather than stopping. Refunds on Pro are 30 days, no questions asked.
