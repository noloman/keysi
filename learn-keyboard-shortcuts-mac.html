Mac keyboard shortcuts

How to learn the keyboard shortcuts of the Mac apps you use

Finding a shortcut is the easy half. You look it up, use it once, and the next day you're back in the menu. This page is about making it stick: what you can do with macOS alone, and then Keysi Pro, the paid part of the app this site sells.

What works without installing anything

  • Start from the commands you already use. Most of an app's shortcuts are for commands you never run. Learn the ones you pick from a menu every day; the menu prints each shortcut next to it.
  • Read the keycaps on the way past. When you pick a command from the menu, look at its shortcut before you let go, and press the keys next time.
  • A few at a time. Pick a handful in the app you use most, not a whole cheat sheet, and add more once those are habit.
  • Give a shortcut to a command that has none. System Settings ▸ Keyboard ▸ Keyboard Shortcuts ▸ App Shortcuts lets you assign keys to any menu command, by its exact menu title.
  • Check the shortcut isn't already taken. macOS handles some key combinations before the app sees them, so a shortcut that does nothing is often taken, not forgotten. How to see any Mac app's keyboard shortcuts covers that, and the three places macOS lists them.

None of that notices when you reach for the mouse for a command that has a shortcut, or brings back the ones you keep missing a few days later.

What Keysi Pro adds

Free Keysi shows every shortcut of the app you're in when you hold ⌘, and runs any of them with a click. Keysi Pro ($14.99, once) teaches them:

  • Coach. When you pick a menu command by hand that has a shortcut, it shows one quiet reminder, then stops mentioning the ones you've started using. A cooldown keeps it from becoming noise; mute one shortcut or all of it in Settings ▸ Coach, or keep it quiet during a Focus.
  • Practice Mode. It drills the ones you keep missing with spaced repetition: get one right and it comes back after a day, then two, four, eight and sixteen.
  • Your own numbers. Keysi times your trips through the menus, counts the times you pressed the shortcut instead, and shows both. Until it has timed enough trips, it shows no time figure. The overlay marks the shortcuts you've learned, and a weekly summary says what stuck.
  • Shortcuts for commands that have none. When you keep clicking a command with no shortcut, Keysi suggests a combination neither the app nor macOS uses, and shows you where to set it.

I built it for the apps people work in all day, like Xcode, Logic, Final Cut, Figma and Excel. Coaching does nothing in web browsers unless you turn that on in Settings; the overlay still works there.

Every install includes 14 days of Pro. After that Keysi drops to the free tier and keeps working, and nothing you set up is deleted. Pro is one payment, not a subscription, for up to three Macs.
