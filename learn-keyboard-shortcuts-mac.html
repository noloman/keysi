Mac keyboard shortcuts

How to learn the keyboard shortcuts of the Mac apps you use

Finding a shortcut is the easy half. You look it up, use it once, and the next day you reach for the menu again. This page is about the other half: what helps a shortcut stick, what you can do with what macOS already gives you, and then Keysi Pro, which is the paid part of the app this site sells.

What works without installing anything

  • Start from the commands you already use. A list of every shortcut in an app is mostly commands you never run. The ones worth learning are the ones you pick from a menu every day, and the app's own menu prints each one's shortcut right next to it.
  • Read the keycaps on the way past. When you do pick a command from the menu, look at its shortcut before you let go, and press the keys the next time instead.
  • A few at a time. Pick a handful in the app you spend your day in, rather than a whole cheat sheet at once, and add more once those are habit.
  • Give a shortcut to a command that has none. System Settings ▸ Keyboard ▸ Keyboard Shortcuts ▸ App Shortcuts lets you assign keys to any menu command, by its exact menu title.
  • Check the shortcut isn't already taken. macOS claims some key combinations for itself and handles them before the app sees them, so a shortcut that does nothing is often taken rather than forgotten. How to see any Mac app's keyboard shortcuts covers that, and the three places macOS lists them.

What none of that does is notice the moment you reached for the mouse for a command that has a shortcut, or bring back the ones you keep missing a few days later. A list waits to be asked.

What Keysi Pro adds

Keysi's free part shows every shortcut of the app you're in when you hold ⌘, and runs any of them with a click. Keysi Pro ($14.99, once) is the part that teaches them:

  • Coach. It notices when you pick a menu command by hand that already has a keyboard shortcut, and offers one quiet, dismissible reminder. It tracks whether the reminder stuck, so it stops mentioning the ones you've started using. A cooldown keeps it from becoming noise; you can mute one shortcut or the whole feature from Settings ▸ Coach, and tell it to stay quiet while a Focus is on.
  • Practice Mode. It drills the ones you keep missing, with spaced repetition: one you get right comes back after a day, then two, four, eight and sixteen.
  • Your own numbers. Keysi times your own trips through the menus and counts the times you pressed the shortcut instead, and shows both side by side. Until it has timed enough trips, it shows no time figure at all. The overlay also marks which shortcuts you've actually learned, and a weekly summary says what stuck.
  • Shortcuts for the commands that have none. When you keep clicking a command with no shortcut, Keysi suggests a key combination neither the app nor macOS uses, and shows you where to add it.

It is built for the work apps you live in all day, like Xcode, Logic, Final Cut, Figma and Excel. Coaching deliberately does nothing in web browsers unless you switch that on in Settings; the overlay still works there.

Every install includes 14 days of Pro. When they run out Keysi drops to the free tier rather than stopping, and nothing you configured is deleted. Pro is one payment, not a subscription, for up to three Macs.
