KeyClu vs Keysi

KeyClu is free. So is Keysi. Here's what the $14.99 adds.

KeyClu is a good app, actively maintained, and free. If all you want is a panel that shows the frontmost app's shortcuts when you ask, use KeyClu. This page is about what's different.

The difference: KeyClu shows. Keysi teaches.

A shortcut reference, KeyClu included, waits to be asked. You realise you don't know a shortcut, open the tool, and read it. That only helps when you already know you're missing something.

The costly case is the one you never notice: going to the menu forty times a week for a command that had a shortcut all along. You don't look it up, because it doesn't occur to you there's anything to look up.

Coach mode is for that. When you pick a menu command by hand that has a shortcut, Keysi shows one quiet reminder you can dismiss, then stops mentioning the ones you've started using. A cooldown keeps it from becoming noise, you can mute it per shortcut or entirely in Settings ▸ Coach, and it keeps a list of what you're still learning so you can work through it.

It also measures whether it worked. Keysi times your own menu trips (from the menu opening to your pick), so it can say "your menu trips typically take 2.4s, and you've skipped 412 of them." Both numbers are counted, not estimated, and both are shown so you can check the arithmetic.

That's why the cheat sheet itself is free here. I'm not going to charge for a reference you can already get for nothing. The $14.99 is for the part that makes the reference unnecessary.

KeyClu's "Learn" feature is not the same thing

The names sound alike. KeyClu's Learn hides shortcuts you already know, so the list shows what's still unfamiliar when you open it. It's a good idea and it works well.

It still only acts when you open KeyClu, and it filters what's shown. Coach acts when you didn't open anything, for the shortcut you weren't going to look up.

The other differences, briefly

  • Click a row to run it. Keysi reads the live menu tree, so clicking a row fires that exact menu action.
  • Conflict warnings. Keysi flags app shortcuts that macOS will intercept first, before you press them.
  • Localization. Keysi ships in 9 languages. KeyClu ships 4 built in (English, German, Chinese and Japanese), with more via community translation.
  • Custom sheets. Keysi ships ten curated sheets (Figma, Slack, Notion, Obsidian, Excel, Word, Ghostty, Vim, tmux and Emacs) and, since 1.0, an editor for your own. Both are free. KeyClu has ready-made sets you import from KeyClu Extensions, lets you register your own shortcuts by hand, and pairs with a separate CustomShortcuts app. Different approaches, both real.

And Keylume, which coaches too

Keysi is not the only app that coaches. Keylume is a menu-bar app that shows the active app's shortcuts while you hold the right ⌘ key, read live from its menu bar, and shows a small toast ("Use ⌘N instead") when you click a menu item that has a shortcut. It also tracks which shortcuts you've adopted and which need practice, runs locally, and says it never logs keystrokes. It's $12 once for 3 Macs, with a 14-day free trial. If those nudges are what you want, it's a good app and the cheaper one. Try it.

What's different, going by Keylume's own site on 2026-10-06:

  • The overlay is free for good. Keylume says it is fully functional for 14 days, after which you buy a licence to keep using it. Keysi's overlay, panel, click-to-run, cheat sheets and Markdown and PDF export are free with no time limit, and its 14 days of Pro end in that free tier, not a locked app.
  • What the $14.99 covers beyond the nudge. Practice Mode with spaced repetition, a suggested key combination for commands you keep clicking that have none, and "New" marks on the shortcuts an app update added.
  • Things Keylume's site doesn't mention. Warnings when macOS or another app's hotkey will take a shortcut first, your own remapped shortcuts read from VS Code, JetBrains IDEs, Xcode, Vim and Emacs, cheat sheets for tools with no menu bar, export, Raycast, Alfred, Shortcuts and terminal integrations, and localisation (Keysi ships in 9 languages). Not mentioned is not the same as absent; check Keylume directly.

Reasons to pick KeyClu instead

  • It's free, and it's not crippled. No upsell, no nag screen, nothing held back. That's rare.
  • You want to register arbitrary custom shortcuts rather than use curated per-app sheets.
  • You're not on macOS 14. Keysi requires macOS 14 or later.
  • You don't want to be told anything. If a reminder popping up sounds annoying, skip Pro; coaching is the point of it. You can turn Coach off, but then you'd be paying for a lookup panel you can get free.

Decide it by running it, not by reading a table

Run Keysi for a week in the app you use most and see how often Coach catches you. If it's rarely, you already know your shortcuts, and KeyClu or Keysi's free tier is the right call. The $14.99 only makes sense if it's more often than you'd like.
