1.0.6 13 September 2026

Eight new features, 10 changes and 17 fixes.

  • There is now a page at keysi.io/integrations.html explaining every way Keysi can be reached from outside itself — the Raycast extension, the Alfred keywords, the keysi terminal command, Shortcuts and Siri, Spotlight and the Services menu — with the setup each one needs. The Integrations window in the app links to it.
  • There is now an Integrations window — in the menu bar, next to Your Keysi Progress — that says what "Keysi, reachable from everywhere else" actually means on your Mac. It lists all six surfaces (Raycast, Alfred, the keysi terminal command, Shortcuts and Siri, Spotlight, and the Services menu), shows which of them are ready and which need a step, and ends each one in an action rather than an instruction. It knows whether you have Raycast or Alfred installed and shows their own icons when you do, and it tells you whether keysi is on your PATH — including the case worth knowing about, where something *else* on your PATH already answers to that name. The terminal setup is a command you copy and run yourself: Keysi will not write into a directory on your PATH, and nothing in this window runs anything to work out what it shows.
  • Your Keysi now includes a map of your own keyboard, with the keys you reach for shaded in — and the modifiers counted separately underneath, which is where most people find something out about themselves. It is built only from the shortcuts Keysi already tracks: the ones it learned from an app's menus and then watched you press. Keysi does not record what you type, and this feature does not change that.
  • The overlay now shows you which of the shortcuts in front of you you've actually been using. Rows the Coach has watched you press carry a small mark that fills in as the shortcut sticks — once or twice, starting to use it, and using it — so the progress Keysi has always tracked is finally visible where you already look, instead of only in Settings. It's part of Keysi Pro, it counts real keypresses only (clicking a row here is the habit Coach exists to replace, so it never counts toward one), and a shortcut you've never pressed stays unmarked rather than covering the panel in empty circles. Nothing about the free overlay changed: with Pro off, every row renders exactly as it did before.
  • The overlay's text size is now its own control, on a slider from 10 to 20 points, separate from the Compact/Comfortable/Large setting that used to carry it. Those were two settings pretending to be one: asking for tighter rows also shrank the type, and asking for bigger type also spread the rows apart, so nobody could have large text on a dense panel. macOS has no system-wide text-size setting for apps, which is why Keysi has its own — and why it is free, and stays free. Settings ▸ Appearance previews the result as you drag. Existing settings carry over unchanged: whatever size your density preset implied is the size you keep.
  • Practice Mode now ends with something to look at. Instead of a bare score, the last screen lists the shortcuts you drilled with their keys drawn as real keycaps — and for the ones you got wrong, the key you actually reached for beside the one you wanted, which is usually the whole explanation. It also tells you where the session landed: a drill counts towards your streak, and now it says so.
  • Your Keysi is now a window of its own, not a list in Settings. The menu bar's *Your Keysi Progress…* opens it: how many shortcuts you have learned, how many days in a row you have used one, a year of your activity drawn as a grid of little keycaps, which apps you have picked them up in, and the ones you are still learning with their chords shown as real keys. Every number on it is counted rather than estimated. Previously that menu item opened the upgrade recap — which is a fine thing to see once, and an odd thing to be shown when you have already bought the app and just wanted to look at your progress.
  • Exporting an app's shortcuts now offers a PNG alongside PDF and Markdown — the same shortcuts laid out as a desktop picture, sized to the screen you are on, with the keys drawn as real keycaps on a dark ground. The shortcuts you actually want are the ones you have not learned yet, which makes the useful place for them somewhere you cannot avoid looking. If an app has more shortcuts than the screen can hold, the picture says how many fit rather than quietly dropping the rest.
  • If you run a browser Keysi doesn't recognise, you can now name it yourself rather than waiting for an update: defaults write me.manulorenzo.Keysi additionalBrowserBundleIDs -array com.example.SomeBrowser. The built-in list also learns Chrome for Testing, which turns up on a lot of developer machines.
  • Practice Mode now shows how you are doing while you do it, and what to learn when you finish. A progress bar across the top fills in as you answer, marking which ones you got and which you missed; a correct answer is acknowledged instead of silently moving on; and the last screen lists every shortcut you missed or skipped, with its keys, instead of showing only a score. Settings ▸ Coach gains a bar showing how many of the shortcuts Keysi is tracking you have learned. Reminders can now appear in the middle of the screen or below the menu bar instead of the bottom-right corner, if the corner has been too easy to ignore.
  • Shortcut Coach, Practice Mode and your statistics now concentrate on the apps you actually work in, and leave browsers alone. Keysi is built for the apps with menu bars deep enough that learning them buys back real time — editors, IDEs, Slack, Office — and a browser is not one of them: learning how to open a tab from the keyboard saves nobody an afternoon. Reminders, drills and the shortcuts counted in Your Keysi now skip Safari, Chrome, Arc, Firefox, Edge, Brave and the rest. Browser shortcuts Keysi had already recorded are hidden rather than deleted, so nothing you built up is lost and switching back restores it all — and Settings ▸ Coach can still erase the lot if you'd rather. The overlay is deliberately unchanged: hold your trigger in any browser and you still get its whole menu bar, exactly as before. If you do want to drill your browser's shortcuts, Settings ▸ Coach ▸ Coach me in browsers too turns the whole thing back on.
  • Shortcut Coach's reminder has been redesigned around the thing it is actually for. The chord is now a full-size keycap on its own line rather than a small one buried inside a sentence, and the generic lightbulb has been replaced by the icon of the app the reminder is about — so a reminder about Figma says "Figma" before you have read a word of it. It can also be sized and moved now: Settings ▸ Coach offers Small, Medium and Large, and any of the four corners. The reminder appears in the corner of your eye while you are looking at another app, which is a different job from reading a cheat sheet — so it gets its own controls rather than following the overlay's, and the corner that is out of the way in a text editor is the busiest part of the window in Xcode or Figma. It still appears on whichever display you are working on. Medium and bottom-right are exactly what it has always been, so nothing changes until you change it.
  • Keysi's windows now use the same translucent Liquid Glass as its shortcut overlay, with the titlebar blending into it rather than sitting on top as a grey bar — the welcome screen, What's New, Send Feedback, the sheet editor, Practice and your progress. They all follow macOS's Reduce Transparency setting, so turning that on makes them solid.
  • Every row in the Integrations window now carries a coloured icon badge. Previously the one app Keysi could find on your Mac showed its real full-colour icon and everything else showed a grey glyph, which read as two designs in one window rather than one list.
  • Keysi's progress screen reads better. The headline no longer opens with what Keysi is doing to you — it says what is true about your own week, which is the thing Keysi Pro then offers to change. The three numbers underneath sit in even columns instead of drifting apart as the figures grow.
  • Your Keysi reads better. The activity grid is labelled with the months it covers, so a gap is a fortnight in July rather than an unexplained pale patch. An app you have not learned anything in yet says "6 watched" instead of "0 of 6", which was the same fact arranged as a telling-off. A brand-new install says what it is waiting for instead of showing three zeroes and an empty year. And "Still learning" no longer appears as an empty heading once you have learned everything Keysi was watching — which was, of all the states to render badly, the best one.
  • Settings ▸ Shortcuts now says what it is for. It lists four built-in sheets — Vim, tmux, Figma, Slack — and said nothing about the thousands of apps Keysi handles without any sheet at all, so it read as the complete list of supported apps. It now explains first: Keysi reads every app's own menus, and sheets exist only for apps whose shortcuts aren't in a menu bar to read.
  • Keysi now looks like Keysi. The app has always been drawn in the macOS default palette — Color.primary on a windowBackgroundColor — while the marketing site it ships from has a warm identity of its own: a paper off-white, a terracotta accent, and keycaps with a real key face. The two read as different products, and the app was the half with no colour of its own. The site's palette is now the app's. Panels and windows sit on warm paper instead of system grey, text is a warm ink rather than a cool near-black, warnings are the site's amber instead of a bare system orange, and the keycap — the most repeated thing Keysi draws, and the most recognisable thing in a screenshot — gets the full key face: a vertical gradient from a lit top to a shaded bottom, a hairline edge, and a lit top rim. Nothing about this is a departure from the platform. Every colour ships Light and Dark variants that are *not* inversions (the dark terracotta is the lighter of the two, which is what HIG asks for), and the ones that carry meaning — borders, secondary and tertiary text, the keycap edge — ship Increase Contrast variants on top of that. Reduce Transparency still turns the glass off, and now gets Keysi's paper rather than an anonymous window fill: that setting asks for opacity, not anonymity. The keycap's gradient drops to a flat face under Increase Contrast, because a gradient is a soft edge and that setting is a request for hard ones. Settings came along with it. The panes sit on the same paper, their explanatory copy is the same warm ink, and the "Open System Settings"–style links follow your accent instead of the fixed system blue they used to paint — which, on a Mac whose accent is anything but blue, was the one part of the app ignoring the choice you made. The section cards themselves stay the platform's: .grouped draws those, and a Settings pane that hand-paints its own rows stops looking like a Settings pane. The status colours moved into the same family. A ready integration is Keysi's sage rather than system green, an invalid licence key or an unparseable sheet is a warm brick red rather than system red, and a conflict is the site's amber. Each one still carries its own glyph, so none of them is told apart by hue alone. One thing deliberately did not change colour: selection. The keyboard cursor, the matched-modifier tint and every other highlight still follow the accent you picked in System Settings. The brand took the surfaces, the edges and the keycaps; the highlight is still yours.
  • The two places Keysi asks you to record a keyboard shortcut — the global hotkey in Settings ▸ Triggers, and a row's keys in the custom sheet editor — are now reachable and usable with VoiceOver and without a mouse. Both were drawn as plain panels with a click handler, so macOS did not consider them controls at all: VoiceOver announced no name, offered no action, and neither field could be reached from the keyboard. They now say what they are, read the recorded shortcut aloud as words ("Shift Command K", not a row of glyphs), and can be activated without a pointer. Keycaps everywhere in the app — the overlay, the Coach reminder, Practice Mode — now carry the spoken form of the shortcut they draw, so a screen reader reads "Shift Command S" wherever one appears rather than a row of symbols it cannot pronounce.
  • The progress screen no longer undercounts the apps Keysi has been watching. It counted them by a field that only exists on shortcuts you have missed since a past update, so a history spanning nine apps could be described as one. Nothing about the history changed — only the counting.
  • Resetting Shortcut Coach's statistics no longer switches the weekly summary off. Settings ▸ Coach ▸ Reset Statistics cleared the history but left the summary's bookmark of it behind, so every week afterwards looked like a quiet one and no summary was ever sent again — until you had built your whole previous total back up, which for a long-time user could be months. Keysi now clears the bookmark with everything else, and recognises a stale one on its own, so a summary that has already gone quiet starts working again by itself.
  • Shortcuts Keysi learned about before a past update now count again. If you had been using Shortcut Coach for a while, those older shortcuts were invisible to Practice Mode — a drill could be one question long next to a list of twenty — and pressing one of them never registered as a press, so they could never be marked learned and never showed up in your progress. Nothing was lost; it is all counted from now on.
  • The buttons on Keysi's progress screen no longer run off the edge of it. At the window's own width the three of them did not fit, so macOS cut the two that mattered down to "I Already Have a…" and "Unlock Keysi Pro…".
  • Adding your own terminal now works. Keysi shows the built-in Vim and tmux sheets by noticing what's running inside a terminal, and for a terminal it doesn't already know about there's a defaults write command to add it by hand. The setting Keysi actually read had a different name from the one that command wrote, so it had never had any effect for anyone who tried it. The names match now, and the documented command is the one that works.
  • Keysi's warm palette now actually changes under Increase Contrast. It was supposed to from the day the palette landed: every colour that carries meaning — the borders, the secondary and tertiary text, the keycap edge — ships a stronger variant for people who have turned that setting on in System Settings ▸ Accessibility ▸ Display. None of them were ever served. The palette asked AppKit which appearance it was drawing in via bestMatch, and bestMatch collapses a high-contrast appearance onto its plain one — so the high-contrast branch was unreachable and everybody got the standard tones. Nothing logged, nothing looked broken, and the only way to see it was to render all four appearances side by side and notice that two pairs were byte- identical. Contrast is now read from the appearance's own name and from the system setting directly, either of which is enough. A test pins the AppKit behaviour that caused it, so the tempting simplification — folding contrast back into the same call that already handles Light and Dark — fails with the reason attached instead of quietly reintroducing it.
  • Keysi's Shortcuts, Spotlight and Siri actions are now translated. Every action title, description, parameter and error message in them had been shipping in English regardless of your language — as had a few lines in Practice Mode, the overlay's result count, and two rows in Settings ▸ Updates. All of them are now in the nine languages Keysi ships.
  • The Integrations window now finds Alfred whether you are running Alfred 5 or an older version that registers itself under a different name. It previously checked one identifier, so an older install would have been told "Alfred isn't installed" while Alfred sat in the Dock — and would have lost its real icon in the list along with it.
  • The progress screen no longer cuts off what Practice Mode does. The longest line in the Keysi Pro list was truncated mid-word at that window's width, so it read "drilling the ones you keep missing — and the ones y…". It wraps now.
  • Exporting an app's shortcuts as a PDF from a Mac in Dark Mode produced a footer that was nearly invisible on the page. The footer line — the one carrying the app name and the date the sheet was taken — was drawn in macOS's secondaryLabelColor. That colour is dynamic: it means "grey against whatever this Mac is set to". Exported in Dark Mode it baked in the *dark-mode* grey, which is a pale one, and a PDF has no appearance to adapt back to. The page is white, so the footer printed as very nearly nothing. In Light Mode the same export was fine, which is why it could go unnoticed. The footer now uses a fixed tone from Keysi's own palette rather than whichever one the exporting Mac happened to be in, so the same sheet exports identically from any Mac.
  • Keysi no longer wakes its own interface once a second while it is waiting for Accessibility permission. The check that polls for it republished its result every time whether or not anything had changed, which redrew every part of Keysi watching that permission.
  • Finishing a practice drill no longer quits Keysi. The summary screen — your score and the shortcuts worth another look — is taller than a question, and growing the window to fit it crashed the app every time, so the end of a drill was unreachable. The summary also now wraps its text instead of cutting it off, and stays on screen when the list is long.
  • Three paid features were reachable without Keysi Pro, and two paid windows were hidden from the people who had bought them. Asking the Services menu "what's the shortcut for this?" worked on the free tier, as did Spotlight search over your custom sheets — which was also never removed when a trial ended. Meanwhile *Your Keysi Progress…* and *Integrations…* were attached to the licence-status line in the menu bar, which only appears during a trial or on the free tier, so anyone holding a licence had no menu route to either window. Asking Siri for your progress showed an upgrade screen even if you had already upgraded, and the "Practice…" button in Settings ▸ Coach did nothing at all. All fixed, and the reminder's size and corner are now correctly editable on any tier, as they were always meant to be.
  • The progress screen no longer says "1 apps" or "1 shortcut presses". Both counts are 1 exactly when you have only just started using Keysi, so the wrong plural was the first thing a new user read there.
  • Everything that opens Settings works again. On macOS 26 the way Keysi asked for its Settings window stopped working, and it failed silently — so keysi:// links did nothing, the activation link you follow after buying did nothing, "I Already Have a Key" on the progress screen did nothing, the overlay's Settings button did nothing, and opening Keysi from a Spotlight result did nothing. Each of those now opens the one page it was always about: your Keysi Pro details for the licence links, General for the overlay button, Shortcuts for a Spotlight result.
  • On a Mac with more than one display, the windows Keysi opens for itself — the Practice drill, Your Keysi, Settings, Integrations, the sheet editor — could appear on a monitor you weren't looking at. They were centred with NSWindow.center(), which centres on "the screen containing the key window". That is the right rule for an ordinary app and useless for this one: Keysi is a menu-bar app that usually has no window open at all, so there was no key window to follow and macOS fell back to whichever display happened to be active last. Measured on a laptop with an external display mounted above it: keysi://practice opened the drill 1,317 points above the top of the screen being worked on — fully on the other monitor. Every route into these windows that isn't a click in Keysi's own menu hits exactly that case: Raycast, the Alfred workflow, the keysi command, Shortcuts, Siri, Spotlight. A drill you have to go looking for is a drill you skip. Keysi now opens them on the screen holding its own key window when it has one, and otherwise on the screen under your pointer — the same rule hold-⌘ has always used to decide where the overlay appears.

1.0.5 12 September 2026

10 new features, one change and six fixes.

  • An Alfred workflow, and a keysi command that ships inside the app, both with three keywords: show, practice, export. Keysi was reachable from Raycast and Shortcuts already; these are the same idea for people who live somewhere else.
  • Settings ▸ Appearance shows a live preview of the overlay at whatever settings you have selected, plus Restore Defaults. "Compact" versus "Comfortable" is a number of points and "before the name" is a column alignment — neither is something you can picture, and the alternative was to change a picker, close Settings, hold your modifier over another app, and compare against a memory.
  • You can now send feedback from inside Keysi — from the menu bar, from Settings, or from a one-line prompt Keysi shows at most once per version once it has actually saved you some menu trips. The form shows you exactly what will be sent before it sends anything, an email address is optional and used only to reply to you, and nothing about the apps or menus Keysi reads ever goes with it. If you are offline, what you wrote is kept and sent the next time Keysi starts.
  • The overlay now answers to Increase Contrast and Reduce Transparency. Keysi's colours already followed Dark Mode and your accent colour, but its opacities and hairline borders were the app's own decisions and no system setting reached them. With Increase Contrast on, the borders and keycaps strengthen; with Reduce Transparency on, the panel drops its glass for an opaque background and keeps an outline strong enough to stand on its own.
  • You can choose where the overlay opens. Settings ▸ Appearance ▸ Opens: centred as it always has been, near the pointer, at the top of the screen, or wherever you last dragged it to. Centred is still the default and nothing changes unless you pick something else — it was the right choice for a laptop, and less so on a display where the middle of the screen is a second of eye travel from the window you were working in.
  • You can choose what order the rows come in — as they appear in the app's own menus, alphabetically, or most used first. Menu order stays the default, because a cheat sheet whose rows sit where the real menu puts them is most of the point. Most Used leaves anything you have never used in menu order, so switching to it on day one changes nothing and the list sorts itself out as you work.
  • The overlay now suggests one shortcut worth learning even in apps Keysi cannot watch you work in — Figma and Slack among them, whose shortcuts never appear in a menu bar. It used to leave that corner of the panel empty in exactly the apps where a suggestion is hardest to come by otherwise.
  • The menu bar now says how many shortcuts are ready to practice and how many days in a row you have used one. It is a number you can go and look at: no notification, no badge, nothing that pings you to protect a streak. Missing a day costs the count and nothing else.
  • Export any app's shortcuts as Markdown or a printable PDF — from the menu bar, from Shortcuts, or from the new keysi terminal command. Your custom cheat sheets are included, so a Vim or Figma sheet prints alongside the app's own menus.
  • Keysi now shows you what changed the first time you open a new version, in a window you can also reach any time from the menu bar. If you skipped a few versions, it shows everything you missed at once. The window resizes and remembers the size you gave it, which matters more than it sounds for a release with a lot in it.
  • Practice Mode works on the first day now. It still drills the shortcuts you keep reaching for the mouse to use, but when it has not watched you miss enough of them yet it fills the rest from the menus of the app you were just in — so it opens with something to do rather than telling you to come back in a week. It leaves out the ones everybody already knows, so you will not be drilled on Command-C, and a session is ten questions, so it ends.
  • Keysi now recovers from an Accessibility permission macOS will never honour. If the entry in System Settings was written by a different copy of Keysi — a beta, a build from source, or an older install — it keeps its switch on and silently never applies, and turning it off and on again cannot fix it. Keysi now recognises that state, explains it, and offers to reset the entry for you. The permission Keysi reports is also live everywhere now: the menu bar item used to show whatever the answer had been when Keysi launched, for the rest of the session.
  • The update window now tells you what is in an update before you install it. Every release until now offered an update with a blank "what's new" pane, which is a poor thing to ask someone to say yes to.
  • Deleting a custom cheat sheet and resetting Shortcut Coach's statistics now ask first — both threw away work permanently on a single click — and a delete that fails now says so instead of looking like it worked. The sheet editor no longer discards a sheet you were part-way through writing when its window is closed, and it can be resized. Activating a licence shows that it is working rather than just greying out the button, and the overlay's search field, its clear button and the hotkey reset button are now reachable with VoiceOver.
  • The progress screen counted practice answers as menu trips you had avoided, which inflated the "time not spent in menus" figure for anyone who had used Practice Mode. It now counts only the shortcuts you pressed while actually working, and says how many of your presses were drills. The whole point of that figure is that it is arithmetic on two things Keysi measured, so it being wrong mattered more than its size.
  • Fifty-two pieces of text that had only ever been English are now translated into all nine languages Keysi ships. Among them: five licence errors, including the ones a buyer sees if a payment is refunded or disputed; both privacy captions in Settings, which are the two places the app explains what it does and does not send; the overlay's secure-input warning; and the row hint VoiceOver reads aloud. They had been added by hand in a way the existing translation check could not see.
  • The welcome window, Practice, What's New, the progress recap, the feedback form and the sheet editor now open in the middle of the screen. They were measured and centred before their contents had been laid out, so each one then grew up and to the right and opened near the top-right corner.

1.0.4 11 September 2026

A security review of the whole app. Nothing here was being exploited, and none of it needed anything from you — but two of them were ways something else on your Mac could have made Keysi act without your say-so, which is worth closing whether or not anyone had noticed.

  • The "Run Menu Command" Shortcuts action now matches a command's name exactly instead of accepting any prefix of it. Asking for "Delete" could previously run "Delete All Messages…" — you name a command and get a longer, more destructive one you never asked for. A trailing "…" is still optional, so "Save As" still finds "Save As…", and a name that matches two commands now reports that rather than guessing between them.
  • Keysi no longer takes orders from any program on your Mac that asks it to come to the front. There is a message a second copy of Keysi sends to the first so that launching it twice does something visible; it was not checked, so anything at all could send it and make Keysi steal your focus and open a window, over and over. Keysi now verifies that the request really came from another copy of itself.
  • The privacy page now lists two things Keysi was sending that it hadn't named: your macOS version alongside app launches, and three counters (shortcuts tracked, learned, and used) alongside the progress recap. Both were always anonymous and neither is new, but that page ends its list with "Nothing else", which is a promise rather than a summary.

1.0.3 11 September 2026

Two fixes to how Keysi Pro is gated outside the app itself. If you don't use the Raycast extension, there is nothing here for you.

  • The Raycast extension's Pro gate now expires on its own. One of its commands — searching your cheat sheets — answers without ever asking Keysi, so it reads a small file Keysi writes to find out whether Pro is unlocked. Keysi only rewrites that file while it's running, and Keysi is a menu bar app people leave quit for weeks, so a trial that ran out while it was closed still read as unlocked. The file now carries the date Pro lapses, and the extension honours it. A purchased licence has no such date and is unaffected — it would be a poor thanks for buying to lock you out of Raycast for not launching the app.
  • keysi://license with no key now opens Settings ▸ License instead of doing nothing. That's the link the extension shows when it tells you a command needs Pro, and it was the one route out of being locked that didn't go anywhere.

1.0.2 11 September 2026

Mostly reach: Keysi is now available from Spotlight, Raycast, and a keysi:// URL, so it no longer depends on you remembering a hotkey. Pro also changed price — see the last entry.

  • Keysi now asks about Pro at the right moment for people whose Coach data arrived slowly. It never shows an empty progress recap — a paywall with nothing in it is worse than none — but it only ever checked whether there was enough to show at launch and at the moment your trial ended. Keysi runs for weeks at a time, so anyone whose data was still thin on the day their trial ended was simply never asked again. It now checks when the data changes, still at most once.
  • The overlay's footer no longer offers free-tier users a "Practice" button that can't practice. Practice Mode is part of Keysi Pro, so on the free tier that button showed the upgrade screen instead — it now says so rather than promising a drill it won't give you.
  • Coach now catches a menu item chosen by holding the right mouse button and releasing over it, not only a left click. This matters more than it sounds: right-click menus turn out to have been within Coach's reach all along — measured in TextEdit, Chrome and Finder — and this was the one way of choosing an item in them that Keysi could not see.
  • A new page on keysi.io lists every cheat sheet Keysi ships — all the shortcuts in each, and the JSON behind it to fork.
  • If you have usage analytics turned on, Keysi now reports apps where macOS refuses to tell it a menu opened. Shortcut Coach can't see mouse-driven menu picks in those apps at all (Safari is the known one), and we had no idea how many others there are. It sends the app's identifier and the error, once per app per session, and nothing else — site/privacy.html has the full wording. Analytics remains off unless you turn it on.
  • Buy buttons now say which screen they came from, so it's possible to tell which ones are worth keeping. No new data is collected from you: the label rides along on the link to the store, the way any campaign link works.
  • Keysi Pro now costs $19.99 instead of $9.99. Still one payment, still three Macs, still every future update at no extra cost, and the cheat sheet is still free with no time limit — the price change touches only the coaching. For comparison, the tool this most resembles charges $19.99 *a year* for three devices, and charges it for the part of Keysi that costs nothing. If you already bought Keysi Pro, nothing changes for you and nothing ever will — it was one payment and it stays bought, including every future update.

1.0.1 11 September 2026

If you are on 1.0.0, update. Two of these matter regardless of whether you ever buy anything.

  • License activation now goes to the real Gumroad storefront. 1.0.0 shipped before the store opened, so it ran against a stand-in gateway that accepted any key — which is what makes a purchased key mean anything.
  • Keysi now tells you when it's running from somewhere macOS won't let it keep its Accessibility permission — a mounted disk image, or the temporary copy macOS makes of an app opened straight from a download. In both cases the permission you grant silently stops applying, which looks like Keysi losing access it was just given. 1.0.0's disk image made this easy to walk into: it had no Applications folder to drag onto, so the obvious move was to run Keysi where it sat.
  • The 1.0.1 disk image carries that Applications folder, and the copy of Keysi inside it now has its notarization ticket attached — 1.0.0's didn't, so first launch needed a working internet connection and could fail outright offline.
  • A mistyped license key now says so, instead of reporting an unexpected server error.

1.0.0 11 September 2026

Initial public release.

  • Hold ⌘ to reveal the frontmost app's keyboard shortcuts, grouped by menu, with breadcrumbs for submenu-nested items
  • Real keycap rendering (⌘⇧S) instead of text notation
  • Click any shortcut row to run it
  • Global hotkey (default ⇧⌘K) for a searchable, interactive panel, with live modifier filtering, ranked search, and full keyboard navigation
  • System-shortcut conflict warnings
