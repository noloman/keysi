It notices when you reach for the mouse

A shortcut reference waits to be asked. Coach doesn't. It watches for menu commands you pick by hand that already have a keyboard shortcut, and offers one quiet, dismissible reminder. Then it tracks whether the reminder stuck, so it stops mentioning the ones you've started using. That is what the $19.99 buys: a reference that works to make itself unnecessary.

A cooldown throttles it so it can't become noise. You can mute one shortcut or the whole feature from Settings ▸ Coach, and it stays quiet during Focus and Do Not Disturb. Practice Mode drills the ones you keep missing. Your progress is measured against your own menu-trip times: Keysi times how long your trips through a menu actually take, so “typically 2.4s, and you've skipped 412 of them” is arithmetic on two numbers it counted rather than an estimate.

Every install includes 14 days. When they run out the app drops to the free tier. It doesn't stop working, and nothing you configured is deleted.

FAQ

Common questions

Why does Keysi need Accessibility access?

To read the menu bar of the app you're currently using, so it can show its keyboard shortcuts. Verbatim, from the permission prompt: "Keysi needs Accessibility access to read the keyboard shortcuts of the app you're using. It never controls your keyboard or mouse without an explicit click on a shortcut row."

Does Keysi send anything anywhere?

Two network calls happen on their own: a license check to Gumroad, and an update check against Keysi's Sparkle appcast. Nothing about your menus, the apps you use, or the shortcuts you press ever leaves your Mac.

Usage analytics are off by default. If you switch them on in Settings, Keysi sends a short list of product events — app launches, which Settings tab you open, the overlay appearing, which shortcut you triggered, and licensing events like a trial starting or an upgrade prompt being shown — to a EU-hosted PostHog instance. Your license key, the amount you paid, and the contents of any menu are never sent. Crash diagnostics are off by default and, when enabled, are generated on-device by Apple's MetricKit and never leave your Mac unless you copy and share them yourself. See the privacy page for the full list.

Why isn't Keysi on the Mac App Store?

The Accessibility API Keysi depends on to read another app's menu structure doesn't work inside the App Sandbox, which every Mac App Store app is required to use. Apple has confirmed this directly. It's a permanent constraint, not a launch-day limitation — every comparable tool (KeyCue, KeyClu, the original CheatSheet) ships the same way, direct and notarized.

So Keysi is signed with a Developer ID certificate and notarized by Apple: it opens normally, no right-click-to-open dance. Updates ship through Sparkle, each one signed with an EdDSA key built into the app and verified before it installs. Don't take our word for the signature — check it yourself once Keysi is installed:

spctl -a -vvv -t install /Applications/Keysi.app
Can I install Keysi with Homebrew?

Yes. Name the cask in full and that is the whole of it — no tapping, no trusting, nothing to add to a config file first:

brew install --cask noloman/keysi/keysi

It installs the same signed, notarized DMG the Download button hands out; the cask is a checksum of that exact file. It lives in Keysi's own tap rather than in Homebrew's cask repository, which requires an app to clear a popularity bar Keysi hasn't cleared yet — a tap installs identically and needs nobody's permission.

Keysi still updates itself through Sparkle, and the cask says so, so Homebrew won't fight the app's own updater or reinstall over the top of a version you already updated to. If you already have Keysi in your Applications folder, add --adopt and Homebrew takes over the copy that's there instead of refusing to overwrite it.

Can I search while holding ⌘?

No, and it's deliberate. A global event monitor can observe your keystrokes but can't consume them, so anything typed while holding ⌘ would still reach the app underneath — Keysi won't install the kind of input tap that could change that. Hold-⌘ shows you everything; it just can't filter it.

Press the global hotkey (⇧⌘K by default) instead: the same content opens as a real interactive panel where search, arrow keys, and Escape all work.

What happens when the Keysi Pro trial ends?

Keysi keeps working. Every install starts with 14 days of Keysi Pro; when that runs out (after a short grace period) the app drops back to the free tier rather than stopping. Holding your modifier still shows every shortcut, clicking a row still runs it, your custom sheets still load. What stops is the coaching: the reminders, Practice Mode, the progress figures and the weekly summary. Nothing you wrote or configured is deleted, and upgrading later picks up where you left off — Keysi keeps counting locally the whole time, which is how it can show you what you missed.

An app I opened shows no shortcuts

This usually happens right after an app launches, or on its Welcome screen, before it has a real menu bar to read yet. Open a real document or project in the app and try again.

Electron apps work: Slack, VS Code and Figma all return correct menu trees. For the bindings that only exist in an app's renderer and never reach its native menu bar, built-in custom sheets fill the gap.

Can I use a modifier other than ⌘ to trigger the overlay?

Yes. Settings ▸ Triggers lets you pick ⌘, ⌥, ⌃ or ⇧ as the hold modifier, and the hold delay is adjustable. Keysi will warn you about the trade-offs of the non-⌘ options — ⌥ also reveals alternate menu items, ⌃ overlaps with secondary click and Full Keyboard Access, and ⇧ can trip Slow Keys or Sticky Keys if you have them on — but it won't stop you choosing one. The global hotkey (default ⇧⌘K) is separately rebindable to any combination you like.
