Privacy

What Keysi does — and doesn't — send anywhere

Last updated with the 1.0.4 release.

The short version

Keysi reads the menu structure of whatever app is frontmost, on your Mac, to show you its keyboard shortcuts. That information never leaves your Mac. Two network calls happen automatically — described in full below — and nothing else does unless you turn it on yourself, or press Send on the feedback form. Crash diagnostics are off by default; if you turn them on in Settings, they're generated on-device by Apple's MetricKit and never transmitted anywhere automatically. Usage analytics — inside the app, and on this website — are opt-in too; see Analytics below for exactly what's collected when you say yes.

Accessibility access

Keysi asks for Accessibility permission the first time it runs. This is what lets it read another app's menu bar structure via macOS's Accessibility API. Verbatim, from the permission prompt itself:

"Keysi needs Accessibility access to read the keyboard shortcuts of the app you're using. It never controls your keyboard or mouse without an explicit click on a shortcut row."

Keysi only ever simulates a click on your explicit action — clicking a shortcut row to run it. It never types on your behalf, never moves your mouse, and never reads what you type into other apps.

The two network calls Keysi makes automatically

  1. License check. When you activate a license, or periodically revalidate one already active, Keysi contacts Gumroad's License API with your license key. ("Deactivate This Mac" itself is local only — it clears the license from this Mac's Keychain without contacting Gumroad.) Gumroad is the merchant of record for your purchase and processes this on Keysi's behalf; see their own privacy policy for how they handle payment and license data.
  2. Update check. Keysi periodically checks a public appcast feed (hosted on GitHub Pages) to see if a new version is available, using Sparkle. This is a standard, unauthenticated HTTP request for a static XML file — no identifying information about you or your Mac is sent with it beyond what any HTTP request includes (like your IP address, visible to the hosting provider).

Nothing else Keysi does touches the network by default. The shortcuts it reads, the apps you use, your custom cheat sheets, and your settings all stay on your Mac — with one opt-in exception, covered next.

Analytics — always opt-in, on and off by default

Two separate analytics systems exist, covering two separate things: this website, and the Keysi app itself. Neither is on unless you actively turn it on, and turning either off again is one click.

This website uses Google Analytics to understand traffic — which pages get visited, what referred you here, and whether the "Buy" button gets clicked. Nothing from Google loads until you click "Accept" on the banner shown on first visit; clicking "Decline" (or simply ignoring the banner) means no Google request is ever made. Google's own privacy policy governs how it handles this data. You can withdraw consent at any time by clearing this site's data in your browser, which brings the banner back on your next visit.

The Keysi app has a "Share usage analytics" toggle in Settings → General, off by default. If you turn it on, Keysi sends a small, disclosed set of anonymous events to PostHog (EU-hosted):

  • App launches — with Keysi's own version number and your macOS version, so we can tell which releases are actually in use and which macOS versions still need supporting — and which Settings tab you open.
  • The overlay showing, and which individual shortcuts you trigger — the shortcut's name and the app it ran in, so we can see which shortcuts and apps matter most to Keysi users.
  • Licensing and upgrade events: a trial starting, moving between the free and Pro tiers, the progress recap being shown and which button you pressed on it, opening the checkout page and which screen you opened it from, a Keysi Pro feature being blocked, and whether a license activation succeeded or failed — the latter with a coarse reason such as "invalid key" or "network".
    The progress recap carries three numbers along with it: how many shortcuts Keysi is tracking for you, how many of those you've learned, and how many times you've used a shortcut instead of the mouse. They are the same three figures the recap itself puts on screen, they are counts rather than any record of which shortcuts, and they are there so we can tell whether people who have got real value out of Keysi upgrade at a different rate from people who haven't.
  • Three things about Shortcut Coach and Practice Mode, because otherwise there is no way to tell whether the paid half of Keysi actually works for anyone: that a reminder was shown, and in which app; that a practice drill finished, with how many questions were asked, how many you got right first time, and how many of them came from the app's own menus rather than from what Coach had watched; and that a shortcut crossed into "learned", with the app's name and how many times you had reached for the mouse for it first. Counts and app names only — never which shortcut, and never what you were doing in it. The "learned" one is sent at most once per shortcut, ever.
  • That you exported a cheat sheet, in which format, and how many shortcuts were in it. Not the app, not the file name, and not where you saved it.
  • That the feedback prompt was shown and how you answered it, that the feedback form was opened and from where (the prompt, the menu bar, or Settings), and that a message was sent, with its category and whether it went out or was queued for later. Never the message itself and never a reply address — those travel only with the message you pressed Send on, described under Feedback below.
  • Whether Keysi warned you that it was running from somewhere macOS won't let it keep its Accessibility permission — a disk image, or a temporary copy — and which of those two it was. No path is sent, only which of the two situations it recognised.
  • When macOS won't tell Keysi that an app has opened a menu — which is exactly what Shortcut Coach needs in order to notice you picking a command with the mouse — the identifier of that app and the error macOS returned. Safari is the one we already know about. This is sent once per app per session, and only for apps where that failed, so we can find out which apps Coach can't help you in yet; it is not a record of the apps you use.

Nothing else. Never your license key, never the amount you paid or any payment detail, never the content of any document, message, or window, never anything from a custom cheat sheet, and never the contents of any menu Keysi reads. Turning the toggle back off stops all future events immediately.

Feedback you send us

Keysi has a feedback form — in the menu bar, in Settings → General, and very occasionally offered by a one-line prompt. Nothing is sent from it unless you press Send, and the form shows you the exact payload first, line by line, above the button. This is the one thing Keysi sends that isn't covered by the analytics toggle: whether that toggle is on or off makes no difference here, because pressing Send is its own decision and it covers exactly one message.

What goes with it:

  • What you wrote, and the category you picked.
  • An email address, only if you typed one in. It is used to answer you and nothing else — never added to a mailing list, and never published anywhere.
  • If you leave the box ticked — and it is shown in the preview either way — your Keysi version and build, your macOS version, your language, whether your licence is a trial or active, and whether Accessibility is granted. Untick it and none of that is sent.

It goes to the same EU-hosted PostHog project as the opt-in analytics, as a single event with a random one-off identifier. Two messages sent from the same Mac cannot be linked to each other, or to anything the analytics toggle collects, and no profile is built from them.

What never goes with it, whatever the boxes say: the app you were using, any menu Keysi has read, anything Shortcut Coach has recorded, your diagnostic log, and your licence key.

A short excerpt of what you write may be quoted in an issue on Keysi's own development tracker, so the problem you reported can be tracked until it's fixed. That tracker is private — only whoever works on Keysi can see it — and your message is not published anywhere. Email addresses, file paths under your home folder, links and anything shaped like a licence key are stripped out of the excerpt even so, and your reply address is never included in it at all.

What's stored locally

Shortcut Coach keeps a count, on your Mac only, of which menu commands you pick with the mouse that already have a keyboard shortcut, and how often you later press that shortcut instead. It also times how long your own menu trips take, so the progress figures Keysi shows you are measured rather than guessed. That counting continues on the free tier, even though the coaching itself is part of Keysi Pro — it is what lets Keysi show you real numbers about your own habits if you ever look. None of it is transmitted anywhere; it lives in ~/Library/Application Support/Keysi/. Turning off "Suggest shortcuts when you click with the mouse" in Settings → Coach stops the counting entirely, and "Reset Statistics" on that same pane deletes what has been collected.

If you send feedback while you're offline, the message waits in ~/Library/Application Support/Keysi/ and goes out the next time Keysi starts with a working connection — or is discarded after a fortnight, unsent. Anything you start writing and don't send is kept in the same place, so closing the window doesn't throw it away; it stays on your Mac until you send it or delete it yourself. Keysi also remembers, on your Mac only, whether it has already asked you for feedback, so that it doesn't ask again.

Your license state, trial state, and preferences (hold delay, hotkey binding, menu bar icon visibility, and so on) are stored locally on your Mac — in the standard macOS locations for app preferences and keychain items — and are never uploaded anywhere by Keysi itself.

Custom cheat sheets

Any custom JSON cheat sheets you add live in ~/Library/Application Support/Keysi/Sheets/ on your own Mac. Keysi never reads, uploads, or shares the contents of that folder with anyone.
